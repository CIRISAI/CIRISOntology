## 1. The classification, with 't Hooft applied per dial

The criterion (G. 't Hooft, Cargèse 1979, NATO ASI B59 (1980) 135) licenses **anomalous
smallness**: a parameter may be very small if setting it to zero increases the theory's
symmetry, because then δα_i ∝ α_i and smallness is stable under the RG flow. It says
nothing about parameters of order one. Applied honestly, the Standard Model's dials sort
into **three** classes, not two.

### 1.1 PROTECTED

**α, the fine-structure constant — protected, but *not* by 't Hooft.**
α ≈ 1/137 is not anomalously small; it is a marginal coupling of order 10⁻². The criterion
does not apply and it is a category error to invoke it here. The real protection is
Wilsonian and gauge-theoretic: exact U(1) gauge invariance forbids a photon mass, so the
electromagnetic sector contains **no relevant operator at all**, and α is exactly marginal —
its only cutoff sensitivity is logarithmic. Nothing has to be cancelled to keep α where it
is. It is the paradigm "marginal survivor," and its stability is a statement about the
renormalisation group, not about a near-symmetry.

**m_e and every charged-fermion mass — protected, strictly by 't Hooft.**
Setting m_e → 0 restores an exact chiral symmetry U(1)_L × U(1)_R acting independently on
the left- and right-handed electron fields. That symmetry forbids an additive mass
correction, so δm_e ∝ m_e ln(Λ/m_e): multiplicative renormalisation, exactly the pay-off the
criterion promises. The electron's smallness relative to the electroweak scale maintains
itself, with no cancellation and no payer. This is the textbook case 't Hooft's paper is
built around, and it is the only class here that satisfies the criterion in its strict form.

**μ = m_p/m_e — protected on both legs, by two *different* mechanisms.**
This dial is worth separating because the page's prose treats protection as one thing and it
is not. The denominator is chirally protected as above. The numerator is not: m_p ≈ Λ_QCD,
and Λ_QCD is not a Lagrangian parameter at all — it is generated by **dimensional
transmutation** from the marginal coupling g_s, Λ_QCD ~ M exp(−c/g_s²(M)). Its enormous
smallness relative to the Planck scale is therefore exponentially cheap: it costs an O(1)
coupling and buys 19 orders of magnitude, and it runs only logarithmically. So μ's stability
rests on chiral symmetry at one end and on asymptotic freedom at the other. Both are real;
they are not the same argument, and the audit does not blur them.

**Quark and lepton mass ratios — protected, strictly by 't Hooft**, inheriting chiral
protection from each mass individually.

**Photon masslessness — protected absolutely**, by exact gauge symmetry rather than a
near-symmetry.

### 1.2 UNPROTECTED

**Λ, the cosmological constant.** Λ is the coefficient of the identity operator — mass
dimension four, the most relevant operator that exists. Setting Λ → 0 restores no symmetry
of the non-supersymmetric Standard Model. (Unbroken supersymmetry would do it; supersymmetry
is broken, and broken SUSY reinstates the problem at the breaking scale.) The cutoff
sensitivity is quartic, and the observed value sits ~120 orders below the naive estimate.
Fails the criterion outright, and by the largest margin in physics.

**m_H², the Higgs mass parameter.** Dimension-2 relevant operator; quadratic cutoff
sensitivity; the cancellation runs to roughly thirty decimal places. Setting m_H² → 0
restores only *classical* scale invariance, and that is anomalous — broken by the running of
the very couplings it would constrain — so it is not a symmetry of the quantum theory and
does not satisfy the criterion. **Declared dissent, registered in advance:** W. A. Bardeen
(FERMILAB-CONF-95-391-T) argues that classical scale invariance broken only logarithmically
*is* a protection. This audit adopts the majority reading. Adopting Bardeen's would move
this row to UNKNOWN-PROTECTION and shrink the unprotected class to a single member.

### 1.3 UNKNOWN-PROTECTION — and this is where the page's prose is wrong

**θ̄_QCD.** Setting θ̄ → 0 restores CP (and P, and T). By the letter of 't Hooft's
criterion, **θ̄ passes** — and the radiative stability is not merely formal: the contribution
to θ̄ induced by the CKM phase is famously of order 10⁻¹⁷, far below the experimental bound,
so a small θ̄ genuinely stays small under quantum corrections. What θ̄ lacks is not
protection against drift; it is an *explanation of its value*, because the symmetry doing the
protecting is one the Standard Model demonstrably violates elsewhere (the CKM phase is
O(1)). The strong-CP problem is therefore a puzzle about an initial condition, not a
maintenance failure.

This distinction — **protection against drift** versus **explanation of value** — is the
audit's main structural finding, and it does not favour the page. The `law-as-habit` prose
currently reads: "the strong-CP angle is tiny without visible protection — which is exactly
why physicists proposed the axion." The second half is fine (Peccei & Quinn 1977; Weinberg
1978; Wilczek 1978 — the axion is a proposed *dynamical relaxation* mechanism, still
unobserved). The first half is not: the protection is visible, it is CP, and it works.

### 1.4 NOT-SCORED

**G, Newton's constant.** Dimensionful. "G varied" is not a unit-independent statement
(M. J. Duff, hep-th/0208093); only dimensionless combinations such as (m_p/M_Pl)² carry
meaning. Excluded from the scorecard by §1.2 of the pre-registration, decided before its
bounds were read. Bounds reported below for completeness.

## 2. The assembled bounds

Rate conversions from a total fractional change Δx/x over lookback time T use
|ẋ/x| ≈ (Δx/x)/T and **assume a constant rate**, which is an assumption and is flagged as
one everywhere it is used. Power tiers are against the frozen yardstick H₀ = 7.3 × 10⁻¹¹ yr⁻¹
with plausible drift ≡ 0.1 H₀ = 7.3 × 10⁻¹² yr⁻¹ and STRONG ≡ B ≤ 10⁻³ H₀ = 7.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ yr⁻¹.
(Exact arithmetic for h = 0.7 gives 7.2 × 10⁻¹¹ yr⁻¹; the frozen value is used as declared,
and the 2% difference changes no tier.)

The strongest bound is **2.9 × 10⁷ times tighter** than the pre-registered plausible-drift
scale. If α were decaying on any timescale within seven orders of magnitude of the age of
the universe, these clocks would have seen it.

### 2.2 α — quasar absorption, including the controversy in full

**The claim.** Webb et al. 2011, PRL **107**, 191101 (arXiv:1008.3907): the combined
Keck/HIRES + VLT/UVES sample fits a **spatial dipole, significant at 4.2σ**, toward
RA 17.5 ± 0.9 h, Dec −58 ± 9°. King et al. 2012, MNRAS **422**, 3370 give the dipole
amplitude as Δα/α ≈ 0.97 (+0.22/−0.20) × 10⁻⁵ — i.e. about 10 ppm. Wilczynska et al. 2020,
Sci. Adv. **6**, eaay9672 add four measurements toward J1120+0641 at z = 7.085,
Δα/α = (−2.18 ± 7.27) × 10⁻⁵ (consistent with no change at that sightline), and report that
combining with existing data still prefers spatial variation over no variation at **3.9σ**.

**The refutation.** Whitmore & Murphy 2015, MNRAS **447**, 446 measured long-range
distortions in the wavelength scales of both HIRES and UVES and found them **ubiquitous and
substantial**, with gradients of order ±200 m s⁻¹ per 1000 Å — enough to produce spurious
Δα/α at the **~10 ppm level**. That is the *same size as the claimed dipole amplitude*, from
an instrumental effect present in both of the instruments that produced it.

**The decisive follow-up.** Murphy et al. 2022, A&A **658**, A123 (arXiv:2112.05819) observed
the best-studied absorber, z = 1.15 toward HE 0515−4414, with ESPRESSO, whose laser frequency
comb removes wavelength calibration error as a systematic:
**Δα/α = 1.3 ± 1.3_stat ± 0.4_sys ppm** — null. The same system with UVES gave
Δα/α = −1.42 ± 0.55_stat ± 0.65_sys ppm (Kotuš, Murphy & Carswell 2017, MNRAS **464**, 3679).

**Scoring, by the frozen §3 rule.** The dipole is a ≥3σ nonzero claim that fails the
un-refuted condition: an instrumental systematic of equal magnitude was identified in both
contributing spectrographs, and the comb-calibrated remeasurement of the best system is null.
Verdict: **DRIFT CLAIMED-DISPUTED**, which §3 fixes as **not confirmed**. The audit takes no
position on who is right; the pre-registered rule decides the row.

**Two honest qualifications.** First, the Webb result is a *spatial* dipole, not a temporal
drift, so treating it as "drift" for kill purposes requires a model. It is scored in-scope
anyway — a protected dial varying across the sky would fail to hold just as surely as one
varying in time — but the conversion to a rate is model-dependent and is not performed.
Second, power for this leg, converting the ESPRESSO null over a z = 1.15 lookback of ≈ 8.4
Gyr: B ≈ 1.4 ppm / 8.4 Gyr ≈ **1.7 × 10⁻¹⁶ yr⁻¹**, B/H₀ ≈ 2.3 × 10⁻⁶ → **STRONG**
(constant-rate assumption flagged).

### 2.5 Quark and lepton mass ratios

**No dedicated drift search of scoring quality exists.** These dials are constrained only
indirectly — through μ, and through Oklo under the model assumption that the resonance shift
is attributable to a specific combination of α and m_q/Λ_QCD. This row is reported as
**COVERED ONLY INDIRECTLY** and contributes no independent pair. Stating it plainly: the

### 2.6 θ̄_QCD

Abel et al. 2020, PRL **124**, 081803 (arXiv:2001.11966), the nEDM experiment at PSI:
d_n = **(0.0 ± 1.1_stat ± 0.2_sys) × 10⁻²⁶ e·cm**, interpreted by the authors as
**|d_n| < 1.8 × 10⁻²⁶ e·cm (90% C.L.)** — both verified against the paper text. Using
d_n ≈ (2.4 ± 0.9) × 10⁻¹⁶ θ̄ e·cm, this bounds **|θ̄| ≲ 10⁻¹⁰**, the value commonly quoted
in the literature as |θ̄| < 1.98 × 10⁻¹⁰ (90% C.L.) under the assumption that θ̄ is the sole
CP-violating source. The paper itself names this "the strong CP problem."

This is a bound on the **value**, not on a rate. No drift search on θ̄ at scoring quality
exists (see arXiv:2204.09694, "Is the θ̄ parameter of QCD constant?", for the question being
asked but not yet answered at this precision). Per §6.4 of the pre-registration, θ̄
contributes **no pair**.

**The dispute, reported as required.** Efstathiou 2025, MNRAS **538**, 875 (arXiv:2408.07175)

### 2.9 Higgs — the default schedule

Buttazzo, Degrassi, Giardino, Giudice, Sala, Salvio & Strumia 2013, JHEP **12**, 089
(arXiv:1307.3536). Quoted from the paper: *"vacuum stability of the SM up to the Planck scale
is excluded at 2.8σ (99.8% C.L. one-sided)"*, with the stability condition
M_h > (129.6 ± 1.5) GeV, equivalently M_t < (171.53 ± 0.42) GeV. The instability scale is
Λ_I = 10¹⁰–10¹² GeV. The decay probability integrated over our past light-cone is, in the
paper's words, "spectacularly small," and the vacuum lifetime vastly exceeds the age of the
universe — metastable, not decaying.

**Scoring by frozen §5:** **DEFAULT SCHEDULED** (metastability at 2.8σ ≥ the 2σ threshold).

**Caveat that must travel with this number:** the verdict is dominated by the top pole mass,
and the paper itself adds a ±0.3 GeV theoretical uncertainty on top of experiment to cover
the ambiguity in relating the measured top mass to the pole mass (an ambiguity of order
Λ_QCD). A 2.8σ verdict resting on a quantity with a definitional ambiguity of that size is
soft, and could move with a better top mass determination.

The second conjunct is not answered and cannot be with present data. "The unprotected ones

This is not a defect of the kill's wording. The kill says "DESI **and its successors**
pinning the dark-energy balance at exactly constant," which is explicitly forward-looking. It
names a future measurement, and that measurement has not been made. The honest statement is
that kill leg (3) is **live but not yet runnable in full**, and what it waits on is
specific: a successor survey constraining |wₐ| below ~0.1, together with a top-mass
determination that flips the stability verdict.

### 3.3 The Higgs register (§6.3)

**SCHEDULE PRESENT.** The one unprotected dial we cannot watch move nonetheless carries a
published default schedule: metastable at 2.8σ, instability scale 10¹⁰–10¹² GeV. Consistent
with what the reading expects of an unprotected dial. **Not motion**, and it is not summed
with Λ's. Consistency only.

### 3.4 The θ̄ register (§6.4) — the finding that cuts against us

θ̄ contributes no pair, but the re-classification analysis pre-registered in §6.4 is worth
reporting because it is unfavourable.

* **If θ̄ is classified as the strict criterion says it should be — PROTECTED** — it adds no
  power to the audit, because no rate measurement on θ̄ exists at scoring quality. Neutral.
* **If θ̄ is classified as the page's prose currently implies — UNPROTECTED** — then the
  unprotected class contains a dial that is not merely rigid but is *the most finely held
  number in the Standard Model*: |θ̄| ≲ 10⁻¹⁰, with no observed motion over the accessible
  record. An unprotected dial sitting perfectly still is **exactly the second conjunct of
  this claim's own kill**. It would be supplied gratis, by our own mis-sorting.

The kill would still not fire, because the first conjunct fails and the conjunction requires
both. But the margin is thinner than the prose implies, and the fix is not rhetorical: θ̄
passes 't Hooft, the protection is CP, and the page should say so.

## 4. Honest limits

**4.1 Nulls are consistency, never support.** House rule 6, applied: *the ordering has not
been caught backwards; the selection reading gains consistency and no support.* This audit
was one-directional by construction and said so before it ran (§0a). The
protected/unprotected sort was composed on 2026-07-26 knowing that α-drift searches come back
null and that Λ is the worst number in physics. Even the "theory-only" classification is not
truly independent of phenomenology: Wilsonian RG and 't Hooft naturalness were themselves
developed by people who knew which parameters were embarrassing.

**4.2 The framework predicts an ordering, not a rate — and this cuts hard.** The reading's
own logic says the oldest, most completely paid-up habits should drift *slowest*. So the
reading predicts, from its own premises, that exactly these bounds should come back null —
which is why a null cannot be evidence for it. Worse, the "STRONG power" ratings in §2 all
depend on the imported yardstick 0.1 H₀. If the plausible drift rate for a fully-amortised
habit is instead many orders below H₀ — which the reading has no way to rule out, because it
supplies no rate — then even a 10⁻¹⁹/yr bound is unpowered and every STRONG label in this
document collapses to UNTESTABLE. The audit cannot settle this, because the framework will
not name a rate. **This is the single thing that would most improve the instrument.**

**4.3 The classes are not commensurable and no metric was invented.** Fractional drift per
year (α, μ), a dimensionless equation-of-state deviation (Λ), and a tunnelling lifetime
inferred from two pole masses (Higgs) are three different observables. Each was scored on its
own natural observable against its own pre-declared threshold. The class-to-class comparison
is therefore qualitative — moving / rigid / not-powered — and nothing stronger should be read
into it.

**4.4 The sample is 2 × 1.** Two protected dials with scoring-quality drift records against
one watchable unprotected dial. The quark/lepton mass ratios are covered only indirectly; the
Higgs is a schedule and not motion; θ̄ has no rate measurement; G is excluded. An "ordering
across two classes" established on a 2 × 1 comparison is a thin result even where it is
clean.

**4.5 Rate conversions assume constancy.** Every astrophysical and geological bound in §2 was
converted to a rate by dividing by lookback time. That assumes the drift, if any, was
uniform. A drift concentrated in an epoch not covered by the measurement would evade all of
them.

**4.6 What this audit did NOT do.** It did not run `lake`, touch the Lean, or alter the
stance. It did not combine independent measurements into tighter numbers. It did not
adjudicate either live controversy (the α dipole, the DES-SN5YR offset) — the frozen rules
did that, and both are reported with their refutations attached.

**4.7 What the page should change, and what it should not.**

**One correction, pre-registered before the numbers were assembled and therefore not a
finding of this audit: θ̄'s protection.** The prose's "the odd one out, the strong-CP angle,
is tiny without visible protection" is wrong on its own terms. θ̄ passes 't Hooft's
criterion; its protecting symmetry is CP; what it lacks is an explanation of its value, not
protection against drift. Correcting it also removes the §3.4 hazard, in which our own
mis-sorting hands the kill half its conjunction.

**And one thing the page already gets right, recorded because the audit went looking to
correct it and could not.** On checking the prose against §1.1, the sort is stated
accurately: the gauge couplings are described as drifting "only logarithmically — the
marginal survivors," the fermion masses via the chiral argument ("set the electron's to zero
and a new symmetry appears"), and the photon as "guarded absolutely by gauge symmetry."
Those are three different mechanisms and the page names each one correctly. The only
imprecision left is framing: all three are presented under 't Hooft's banner, when the
gauge-coupling row is really Wilson's marginality rather than 't Hooft's criterion — which
does not apply to a coupling that is not anomalously small. That is a sentence of
attribution, not an error in the physics, and the page's substance survives it intact.

**What the audit did not find any reason to change:** the two-member unprotected class
(Λ, Higgs), the thawing expectation for Λ, and the metastability reading of the Higgs. All
three are stated at the strength the literature supports.
