## S1 — NEW proved claim: `eleven-are-rigid`

- **headline**: The eleven are not a list that could have been shuffled: of the forty
  million ways to relabel them, twenty-four survive the structure, and four survive it
  once grounding order is added.
- **plain**: A worry you should have about any taxonomy: could the names have been
  swapped around and nothing would change? For this one the answer is computed, not
  argued. Ask which relabelings of the eleven kinds preserve the structure they were
  derived from — which speech act each belongs to, and which of them is its family's
  surface. Of the 39,916,800 possible relabelings, exactly **24** survive. Add the
  grounding order — that assumptions found rules, rules derive facts, and hedges modulate
  facts — and exactly **4** survive: swap Priorities with Process, swap Structure with
  Circumstances, and nothing else. Those two swaps are the theory admitting where it is
  genuinely twinned. Everything else about the eleven is forced by their relations.
- **status**: proved
- **witness**: `aut_without_stack`, `aut_without_stack_card`, `aut_with_stack`,
  `aut_with_stack_card`, `aut_without_stack_complete`, `aut_with_stack_complete`
- **confidence**: Proved about the MODEL, and the number is only as strong as the
  structure fed to it — force-membership, surface-status, grounding order. A critic who
  rejects the grounding order gets 24 rather than 4, and both are stated. The two
  surviving swaps are a real limit, not a rounding error.
- **kill**: Read the Lean: exhibit a structure the enumeration should have preserved and
  did not (which would shrink the group and strengthen the claim, so this is the friendly
  attack); or show that one of the four surviving permutations is NOT a symmetry of
  anything the taxonomy cares about, meaning the preserved structure is under-specified.

## S2 — NEW measured claim: `strength-is-content-for-rules`

- **headline**: The taxonomy's lopsidedness is forced, not an omission: changing how
  strongly a rule binds changes what counts as compliance, so it is a change of Rules —
  every one of twelve test items said so.
- **plain**: The eleven kinds are unevenly distributed across their four families —
  claiming has four members, requiring has three. That asymmetry looked like a hole:
  claims can be hedged (Confidence), so why can't requirements? We staked the answer
  before writing a single test item. If must-versus-should reads as a change in
  confidence, a twelfth kind is missing and our own exhaustiveness theorem is in trouble.
  If it reads as a change in the rule itself, the asymmetry is forced by something real:
  hedging a claim leaves what is TRUE untouched, but softening a requirement changes what
  counts as OBEYING it. Twelve items where only the modal word moved: **twelve out of
  twelve read as Rules, zero as Confidence.**
- **status**: measured
- **basis**: `CONJUGATION_TEST_PREREG.md` / `CONJUGATION_TEST_RESULTS.md` (frozen before
  items existed; 12 items x 3 judge models; zero ties, zero voids, zero off-taxonomy).
- **confidence**: Small and decisive — the maximum reading against a threshold of nine,
  with the opposite outcome pre-committed to be reported as a live challenge to the
  eleven. Model panel only; the human ceiling is owed here as everywhere.
- **kill**: A corpus of deontic-strength changes on which a panel (or human annotators at
  ceiling) reads Confidence on a third or more; or a demonstration that softening a
  requirement can leave compliance-conditions unchanged, which would restore the missing
  site.
