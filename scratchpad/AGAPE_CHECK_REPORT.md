# Nygren verification report

Verification target: the staked claim that Anders Nygren (*Agape and Eros*, 1930–36)
structurally distinguishes agape from eros as — eros ACQUISITIVE, motivated by the value its
object already has; agape SPONTANEOUS and UNCONDITIONED ("unmotivated"), indifferent to the
object's existing value, and CREATING VALUE IN ITS OBJECT rather than recognizing it.

Method: web + source retrieval only, no local files consulted. Sources listed at the end.

---

## VERDICT: **CONFIRMED**, with three refinements

Every structural point in the staked claim is correct. Nothing needs retracting. Three
adjustments before anything rests on it:

- **A. There are four marks, not three.** The claim states marks 1–3 and omits mark 4 (agape
  is the initiator of fellowship with God). Mark 4 is theologically load-bearing for Nygren
  and is the one mark that does *not* survive translation into a secular structural reading.
  Drop it knowingly, not silently.
- **B. "Unmotivated" and "indifferent to value" are separate marks in Nygren; the claim fuses
  them.** Keep them apart: critics attack them by different routes, and marks 2 and 3 conflict
  with each other (below).
- **C. "Acquisitive" is Nygren's own word**, not a modern gloss — verified at p. 175 and p. 210.

## 1. The four marks

*Agape and Eros*, Watson translation 1953, "The Content of the Idea of Agape", pp. 75–81:

| # | Mark | Nygren's terms |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Spontaneous and unmotivated** | "Motivated love is human; spontaneous and unmotivated love is Divine." |
| 2 | **Indifferent to value** | "It is only when all thought of the worthiness of the object is abandoned that we can understand what Agape is." |
| 3 | **Creative** | "Agape loves and imparts value by loving. The man who is loved by God has no value in himself; what gives him value is precisely the fact that God loves him." |
| 4 | **Initiator of fellowship with God** | "In the relations between God and man, the initiative in establishing fellowship lies with Divine Agape." |

**The eros side.** Nygren's three characteristics of eros (p. 175): (i) the "love of desire,"
or acquisitive love; (ii) man's way to the Divine; (iii) egocentric love.

The p. 210 summary, in Tollefsen's close paraphrase carrying the page cite:

> "Eros is acquisitive desire and longing. It is man's way to God. It is man's own effort and
> assumes that his salvation is his own work. Eros is egoistic love, a form of self-assertion.
> It seeks to gain its life as a kind of divine and immortalized life. It is man's love and it
> is motivated by the worth or quality of its object.
>
> Agape, on the other hand, is sacrificial giving. It comes from God and is God's way to man.
> Agape is divine grace and is totally unselfish love. It is God's love. Agape is not motivated
> by any value or quality its object might have. It rather creates value."

**Verification limit — read this before quoting.** I could not open p. 210 verbatim
(archive.org's search-inside API returned empty for both scanned copies; two other sources
403'd). The table above is a scholarly paraphrase citing p. 210, independently corroborated.
The famous closing couplet — *"Eros recognises value in its object — and loves it. / Agape
loves — and creates value in its object."* — is **content-confirmed several times over but not
word-confirmed by me**. Quote the content, not the couplet, unless someone opens the book.

**Internal tension to carry.** Marks 2 and 3 pull against each other: agape creates something
it is by definition indifferent to. Every critic works this seam. **If mark 3 is imported,
mark 2 must not ride along unnoticed.**

## 2. Criticisms — the split is contested mainstream, not settled

- **M.C. D'Arcy**, *The Mind and Heart of Love* (1945) — in "the elimination of Eros man has
  been eliminated."
- **John Burnaby**, *Amor Dei* (1938) — with D'Arcy, the first full-length rebuttals. Nygren's
  published reply was that they "start from different premises," i.e. he declined to engage.
- **Gene Outka**, *Agape: An Ethical Analysis* (1972) — the sharpest analytic rejection;
  recasts agape as **equal regard** ("the attribution to everyone alike of an irreducible worth
  and dignity") rather than value-blindness, and restores a place for self-regard. Offers as
  reductio: "a love expressly devoid of anything human and personal is, ipso facto, divine."
- **Catherine Osborne**, *Eros Unveiled: Plato and the God of Love* (OUP 1994) — the
  classicist's demolition. Eros and agape "do not have the determinate meaning accorded by
  Anders Nygren"; **Origen used them interchangeably**; Plato's eros need not be acquisitive
  at all. The BMCR review endorses the anti-Nygren case while faulting her own residual dualism.
- **Torstein Tollefsen** (Univ. of Oslo, 2021) — four charges: (i) *motivforskning* is intuition
  dressed as empiricism and probably reads back Lutheran *sola fide / sola gratia*; (ii)
  emptying the creature of value destroys the doctrine of creation; (iii) the Christology is
  thin; (iv) the NT exegesis inverts its own texts — the Synoptics explain God's love *by
  analogy with human love* (Matt 7:9–11, Luke 15). Closing line: "If Nygren's agape resembles
  his description of it, to call it by the name of love is counterintuitive."
- **Valerie Saiving**, "The Human Situation: A Feminine View" (1960) — the feminist critique,
  and the one with the sharpest practical edge: a norm of total self-emptying is written from
  male experience and licenses exactly the self-negation that is women's characteristic failure
  mode, not their temptation. Called the first landmark in feminist theology.
- **Benedict XVI**, *Deus Caritas Est* (2005) — "eros and agape — ascending love and descending
  love — can never be completely separated." An institutional rejection of the dichotomy.
- **C.S. Lewis**, *The Four Loves* (1960) — a conscious showdown (Nygren is not named in the
  text). Lewis refuses the denigration of Need-love: "man's love for God, from the very nature
  of the case, must always be very largely… a Need-love."
- **Method critics generally** — the objection that motif research "presupposes that one can
  delineate 'pure' concepts or ideas or motifs in history," and that it risks escalating "the
  human being out of history… to a world of clear and untroubled concepts."

## 3. Lexicographic verdict: **a construction, not an inheritance**

The four-fold as a *contrastive system* was never living Greek. The framing must be corrected.

- **agapē (noun)**: no certain attestation in classical Greek. The verb *agapaō* exists but is
  weakly differentiated — "to greet with affection," "to be fond of," "to be contented with."
  The noun is essentially a Hellenistic / Septuagint item. "There is nothing remarkable about
  the noun agapē in any of its pre-Christian uses."
- **Robert Joly (1968)**, *Le vocabulaire chrétien de l'amour est-il original? Philein et agapan
  dans le grec antique* — the decisive philological study, and its answer is **no**: those who
  made Jesus speak Greek "lent him the verb agapao because it was by far the most common at the
  time, both in paganism and elsewhere." Ordinary language drift, not coinage.
- **The Septuagint kills the "unconditional love" semantics outright**: *agapē / agapaō* is the
  standard rendering of Hebrew *ʾāhab* across its full range — including, at 2 Samuel 13,
  **Amnon's rape of Tamar**.
- **D.A. Carson**, *Exegetical Fallacies*: *agapaō* and *phileō* are used interchangeably of the
  Father's love for the Son (John 3:35 vs. John 5:20) with no discernible shift in meaning. The
  John 21 sermon distinction is a semantic fallacy.
- **Osborne**: the vocabulary does not track the concepts even in the Fathers.
- **storgē**: a genuine classical word, but rare as a noun and mostly surfacing in the compound
  *philostorgos*. Never a term in a contrastive system.
- **C.S. Lewis**: builds his own scheme and says so. His organizing axis is **Need-love vs
  Gift-love**, arrived at by reflection, with the Greek words attached afterward as convenient
  labels. He offers no vocabulary lesson and claims no ancient authority. The popular "four
  Greek loves" scheme is downstream of Lewis (1960), not upstream of him.

**Consequence for the page.** "A lost word from the dawn of language" is **false and should be
struck.** "A distinction built by a tradition" is true — and is the stronger claim anyway: it
means someone *did the work*, which is citable prior art rather than a fact of nature one would
merely be rediscovering.

## 4. Four neutral structural profiles (for blind-matching)

**EROS.** Structurally a *lack-driven ascent*. The lover is in a deficit state; the object holds
something the lover does not have; the relation is directed from the deficient toward the
sufficient. Value is **prior and external** — it resides in the object and explains the loving.
In Plato's *Symposium* Eros is the child of Poros (resource) and Penia (poverty), hence "neither
wise nor ignorant" but permanently intermediate and inventive. Diotima's ladder makes the
*object*-series ascend — one body, all bodies, souls, practices and laws, knowledge, Beauty
itself — while the *lover's structure stays constant*: at every rung the same deficit reaching
for the same sufficiency. Substitution of the object is not betrayal but progress. The terminal
state would be possession, which would end the relation.

**PHILIA.** Structurally a *reciprocal, mutually-known bond that requires ongoing activity to
persist*. Aristotle's conditions (*NE* VIII): each must wish the other's good; each must wish it
*for one of three reasons* — utility, pleasure, or the other's character; and **each must be
aware of the other's wishing**. That awareness condition makes philia irreducibly two-place —
goodwill alone is not philia. Two of the three kinds are "accidental": the friend is loved "not
as being the person or character that he is, but as the source of some good or some pleasure,"
so the bond dissolves when its source does. Only character-friendship tracks the person.
Crucially philia is a **maintained** state: "there is nothing so characteristic of friendship as
living together," and "distance does not destroy friendship simply, but the manifestation of
friendship. But if the absence be prolonged, it is thought to obliterate even friendship."

**STORGĒ.** Structurally *unchosen, given, low-selectivity attachment along an existing
relation*. It does not originate in appraisal (no merit is assessed), nor in reciprocal
recognition (no mutual-awareness condition), nor in deficit (no ascent). Its ground is
*proximity plus givenness* — kin, household, the long-familiar. Its typical direction is
asymmetric and generational (parent to offspring, and back). Because it is not selected on
properties it is unusually robust to the object's failures and unusually resistant to
substitution; because it is not chosen, it is the one profile that can obtain without
endorsement.

**AGAPĒ (as constructed by Nygren).** Structurally *unconditioned, value-generating,
source-initiated giving*. It is not triggered by any property of its object (mark 1), takes no
reading of the object's worth (mark 2), and **confers** worth on the object by the act of loving
(mark 3) — value is **posterior and internal to the relation**, the exact inversion of eros.
Direction is downward, from sufficiency to deficiency, and initiative lies wholly with the giver
(mark 4). The object is not a reason but a recipient. Two structural costs critics press: with
no property-sensitivity it is hard to see how agape loves *this* individual rather than anyone,
and marks 2 and 3 jointly require indifference to something the act itself produces.

## 5. Prior art

### 5a. Love creates value in its object

| Source | Content | Proximity |
|---|---|---|
| **Irving Singer**, *The Nature of Love* (3 vols) — **appraisal vs bestowal** | The formal analytic version of exactly this axis. *Appraisal* discovers value already in the object; *bestowal* is "an engendering of value by means of the relationship we have established… surpasses existing values, generating something new." Singer explicitly locates bestowal in the agapē tradition and calls it love's "fine gold thread." | **VERY HIGH — closest thing to a formalization that exists** |
| **Kierkegaard**, *Works of Love* (1847), ch. "Love Builds Up" | **Pre-dates Nygren and is upstream of him.** "The love that builds up has only one mode of progression — to presuppose love"; love is "loved forth" in the other by being presupposed present as the ground. Value-creation-by-attention, stated a century early. | **VERY HIGH** |
| **Harry Frankfurt**, *The Reasons of Love* (2004) | "It is not because I have noticed their value that I love my children… it is on account of his love for them that they have acquired a value they would not otherwise possess." Also supplies the identity-constituting side: loves define the person and set the framework of aims. | **VERY HIGH** |
| **Iris Murdoch** (*The Sovereignty of Good*) / **Simone Weil** | Attention as "a just and loving gaze directed upon an individual reality"; for Weil "morality was a matter of attention, not of will." The attention→reality route rather than the love→value route. | HIGH |
| **Alan Soble**, *The Structure of Love* | Names the axis cleanly: *erosic* love is property-based, reason-dependent, object-centric; *agapic* love is none of those. Soble argues love is **erosic** — i.e. he takes the other side. | HIGH |
| **J. David Velleman**, "Love as a Moral Emotion" | **The principled counterposition**: love responds to dignity already present (Kantian appraisal). Cite so the wager has a live opponent. | HIGH (as opposition) |
| **Josef Pieper**, *About Love* | Love as approval of being: "It's good that you exist; it's good that you are in the world!" Ratification rather than appraisal. | MEDIUM–HIGH |

### 5b. Love as maintenance

| Source | Content | Proximity |
|---|---|---|
| **Canary & Stafford**, relational maintenance theory (1991–) | Literally the science named "maintenance": five behaviors (positivity, assurances, openness, social networks, shared tasks) keeping a relation in a satisfactory state. **Key finding: maintenance strategies "must be enacted in a consistent and continuous manner because their effectiveness diminishes daily."** That is the rent clause in social-psych clothing — decay is free, holding costs payment. | **VERY HIGH** |
| **Joan Tronto**, *Moral Boundaries* — phases of care | A process decomposition with a distinct virtue per phase: *caring about* (attentiveness) → *taking care of* (responsibility) → *care-giving* (competence) → *care-receiving* (responsiveness), later + *caring with* (solidarity/trust). Note **care-receiving is a feedback/audit step** — the receiver's response is what establishes that the care was adequate. | **VERY HIGH** |
| **John Bowlby**, *Attachment and Loss* (1969/1982) | Attachment as a **cybernetic control system with a set-goal**: monitors proximity, compares to desired level, goal-corrects continuously. Formal maintenance in control-theoretic language, decades before the rest. | **VERY HIGH** |
| **Aristotle**, *NE* VIII.5 | The oldest statement of the thesis: friendship is a *hexis* that decays without activity; prolonged absence obliterates the state itself, not merely its exercise. | HIGH (and priority) |
| **Coan & Sbarra**, Social Baseline Theory | Relationships as **bioenergetic**: proximity reduces the metabolic cost of vigilance and self-regulation via risk distribution and load sharing; "the brain construes social resources as bioenergetic resources, much like oxygen or glucose." The closest thing here to a ledger. | HIGH |
| **John Gottman**, repair attempts | Failed repair attempts as a divorce predictor — maintenance framed around a **repair** operation specifically. | MEDIUM–HIGH |
| **Erich Fromm**, *The Art of Loving* (1956) | Love as an activity, "a 'standing in' not a 'falling for'"; four interdependent components — care, responsibility, respect, knowledge. **M. Scott Peck** ("the will to extend one's self for the purpose of nurturing… spiritual growth") and **bell hooks** are downstream. | MEDIUM–HIGH |
| **Maturana & Verden-Zöller**, *The Origin of Humanness in the Biology of Love* | Love defined operationally and non-sentimentally: "the domain of those relational behaviors through which another arises as a **legitimate other** in coexistence with oneself." Behavior-domain, not feeling. | MEDIUM–HIGH |
| **Hanne De Jaegher**, "Loving and knowing: reflections for an engaged epistemology" | Loving and knowing entail each other; knowing is an activity *in the relation* between knower and known. | MEDIUM |

### 5c. The hit worth naming specifically

**C.S. Peirce, "Evolutionary Love" (*The Monist* 3, 1893) — agapasm.** Peirce sets out three
modes of evolution: *tychasm* (chance), *anancasm* (mechanical necessity), and **agapasm**
(creative love) — "a cosmic principle of love throughout the universe creatively supporting the
formation of new evolutionary forms," in which "advance takes place by virtue of a positive
sympathy among the created springing from continuity of mind." He holds tychasm and anancasm to
be *degenerate forms* of agapasm. Given that this repo already runs on Peirce for law-as-habit,
this is the same author formalizing agape as the generative principle inside the same cosmology.
**Proximity: HIGH, and unusually cheap to credit — he is already cited.**

## 6. Flags before anything rests on this

1. **Strike "lost word from the dawn of language."** Falsified by Joly, Carson, Osborne, and the
   Septuagint's Amnon. Replace with "a distinction built by a tradition."
2. **Do not take marks 2 and 3 as a package** — they conflict, and the conflict is where every
   critic goes.
3. **The dichotomy is contested mainstream, not settled.** Anything built on Nygren should carry
   that. Osborne and Tollefsen are the cheapest citations for it.
4. **Two convergences to note without over-reading**: Canary & Stafford's daily-decay claim and
   Coan & Sbarra's metabolic load-sharing both rhyme with the rent clause. Neither is a measured
   decay law — they are qualitative findings. Corroborative framing, not measurement, and by
   this project's own rule 6, not support.
