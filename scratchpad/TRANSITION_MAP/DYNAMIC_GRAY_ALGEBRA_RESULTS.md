## The counterexample that matters

For `RANK1-GENERIC`, the bath is generated by just one time-dependent scalar process multiplying one fixed spatial profile:

`D(t) = xi(t) diag(a)`, with all `a_i` distinct.

The instantaneous bright-to-dark drive is one-dimensional:

`q = Q_dark diag(a)|B>`.

So a covariance-rank or first-order analysis says “one gray direction.” But repeated action gives vectors proportional to

`|B>, a .* |B>, a^2 .* |B>, ...`.

With N distinct `a_i`, polynomial interpolation separates all N emitters. The invariant emitter sector is therefore N-dimensional; including the cavity gives N+1. The numerical closure independently recovered exactly 16, 32, 64 and 128 dimensions.

This is the dynamic analogue of the static benchmark's lesson: a cheap-looking rank can characterize the **first step** while missing the full reachable algebra.

## Common/grouped/local bath interpolation

The controls give a clean continuum between the two classic polariton bath limits:

- COMMON: one identical bath profile on all molecules → no direct gray activation and a 2-state cavity+bright invariant system sector.
- GROUP-G: G exactly bath-equivalent molecular classes → exact system-sector dimension `G+1`.
- LOCAL: independently addressable local profiles → direct gray rank `N-1` and full `N+1` system sector.

The exact compression variable is therefore not the number of stochastic bath processes by itself. It is the number of **distinct complete emitter coupling profiles**, or an approximate analogue when profiles are only nearly equal.

## Physics / chemistry reading

This sharply matches established molecular-polariton physics without claiming it as new phenomenology. del Pino, Feist & García-Vidal (2015) showed that a common long-range phonon bath leaves the bright state effectively decoupled from dark states, whereas independent/local baths induce bright↔dark transfer. The current result supplies the finite-algebra interpolation: exact bath-equivalence classes determine the exact invariant collective sector.

The 2026 MPS–HEOM work finds dynamic disorder harder than static disorder precisely because non-collective degrees are dynamically activated and the bright→dark transfer is controlled by phonon timescales. The algebra result explains why simply factorizing the bath into a small number of temporal correlation functions is not, by itself, enough to guarantee a small system sector.

## SOTA consequence

The naive next proposal is CLOSED:

`low covariance rank => low-dimensional soft-symmetry solver`

is false, even at profile rank 1.

A credible compiler must instead target one of:

1. **exact or approximate bath-equivalence classes** — cluster emitters by their *entire* system-bath coupling profile, not by covariance rank alone;
2. **finite-time approximate algebra closure** — truncate the polynomial/operator closure at an error-controlled degree before it reaches N;
3. **tensor entanglement/compressibility** of the enlarged HEOM/HOPS state, which can remain low even when exact algebraic dimension is extensive;
4. combinations of those with existing MPS/TTN-HEOM methods.

The most promising physics question is now not merely computational: how does the **spatial correlation structure** of molecular phonon environments interpolate between common-bath protection and independent-bath dark-state activation, and how does that control the thermodynamic convergence scale `N_T`? The common/independent extremes are established; the correlation-structure dependence is the natural place to measure before proposing a solver advantage.
