# DARK-STATE SIMULATION / SOTA MAP — PHYS-K11 follow-on

Status: research note on `research/dark-state-sim-sota`; no stance claim. The exact K11 theorem is already in `Core/DarkState.lean`. This note asks what is *not* already known, what can be measured next, and where the structure may buy a simulation advantage.

## 0. Correction before extension

Three symmetry-breaking quantities must remain separate.

1. **Conjugation defect**
   \[
   \Delta_\sigma(H)=\lVert H-P_\sigma H P_\sigma\rVert_F.
   \]
2. **Instantaneous dark-to-bright coupling** for the normalized odd twin state `d`
   \[
   g_{DB}=\lVert(I-|d\rangle\langle d|)H|d\rangle\rVert.
   \]
3. **Spectral impurity**, the observable actually used in PHYS-K11-1 S1b,
   \[
   L_{spec}=1-\max_j |\langle v_j|d\rangle|^2.
   \]

For Hermitian `H` and one transposition, (1) and (2) satisfy the exact identity
\[
  g_{DB}=\Delta_\sigma/(2\sqrt 2).
\]
This does **not** identify either quantity with (3). `L_spec` depends also on spectral denominators / resonances. Therefore the earlier temptation to turn the corpus's 5.8% / 19.2% lifting values directly into a probability-loss ratio is rejected.

PHYS-K11-1 measured `L_spec`, not `Delta_sigma`: CUR-P2 gave 0.054 for Priorities/Process and 0.625 for Structure/Circumstances (11.5x); CUR-SP replicated the sign at 0.411 vs 0.664. The new defect/coupling identity is a new diagnostic to add alongside that result.

## 1. What the exact theorem is in physics language

`DarkState.lean` proves the finite-graph core: if a transposition of twins `(a,b)` is a symmetry, then the odd combination `e_a-e_b` is an exact eigenvector and every non-twin row annihilates it. This is a **compact localized state (CLS) generated by local permutation symmetry**.

That structural mechanism is prior art, not ours. Röntgen, Morfonios & Schmelcher, *Phys. Rev. B* **97**, 035161 (2018), show that local site-permutation symmetries block-partition discrete Hamiltonians into compact-localized and extended sectors and thereby reduce diagonalization to smaller matrices. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevB.97.035161; arXiv:1709.07806. Röntgen et al., *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **123**, 080504 (2019), use such CLSs for quantum-network storage/transfer. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.123.080504; arXiv:1811.02950.

**Consequence for novelty discipline:** do not claim discovery of symmetry-induced dark/compact-localized states or symmetry block diagonalization. The K11 contribution is an independently mechanized instance on a measured semantic coupling graph plus the measured *soft breaking* question below.

## 2. Closest physical analogue for controlled breaking: symmetry-protected BIC -> quasi-BIC

A symmetry-protected bound state in the continuum is exactly dark to an open radiation channel while the protecting symmetry holds. A small asymmetry opens the channel and produces a quasi-BIC. In the usual perturbative regime the radiation amplitude is first order in the asymmetry and the radiative loss is second order; correspondingly many metasurface designs show `Q proportional to alpha^-2`.

This is the cleanest physical cousin of the K11 identity:

- exact twin symmetry -> exact dark sector;
- symmetry defect -> dark/bright amplitude coupling;
- probability / linewidth-like loss -> quadratic at weak breaking.

Representative current engineering literature: asymmetric-cross quasi-BIC metasurfaces report the inverse-square Q/asymmetry relation; see e.g. work summarized under symmetry-protected quasi-BIC design in 2024–2025 photonics literature. This is an **analogy of perturbative structure**, not an identification: a finite Hermitian graph has no radiative continuum unless one is explicitly attached.

### Open / useful question

Can the K11 soft-breaking observable predict a quasi-BIC-like lifetime once the graph is coupled to a sink or bath? The correct experiment is to attach a controlled continuum / Lindblad loss only to the bright sector and ask whether the dark-state decay rate follows the expected quadratic law in `Delta_sigma`, and when spectral resonances destroy the naive perturbative coefficient.

## 3. Chemistry opportunity: dark -> gray states in molecular polaritonics

This is the most concrete current chemistry connection.

Li, Venkatesh, Shi & Montoya-Castillo, 2026 preprint, **"For molecular polaritons, disorder and phonon timescales control the activation of dark states in the thermodynamic limit"** (arXiv:2603.06868), studies exactly the hard regime suggested by our soft-symmetry question: a collectively bright sector plus an extensive dark manifold, with static/dynamic disorder and non-Markovian environments. Their hybrid MPS–HEOM treatment reaches from few emitters toward the thermodynamic limit and finds that bright-to-dark transfer and dark/gray-state participation govern convergence with emitter number.

The overlap is structural:

- identical / equivalent emitters create a large symmetry-reduced bright/dark decomposition;
- disorder breaks permutation symmetry and makes nominally dark states gray;
- bath timescales control transfer between those sectors;
- brute-force open-system state spaces grow rapidly with emitter number.

### Simulation opportunity

Precondition a polariton solver with a **symmetry/near-symmetry compiler** before applying tensor-network or HEOM machinery:

1. detect exact permutation orbits and near-twin clusters from the system Hamiltonian;
2. transform into irreducible bright/dark coordinates;
3. remove exact dark blocks from the expensive bright-sector calculation when allowed by observables and dissipators;
4. retain only the disorder-induced dark<->bright coupling block for weakly broken clusters;
5. rank / compress that coupling (SVD or Krylov reachability) before MPS/HEOM evolution;
6. explicitly transform jump operators and bath couplings too — Hamiltonian darkness alone does not imply a decoherence-free Lindblad sector.

**Credible SOTA claim only after benchmark:** if this reduces bond dimension, HEOM auxiliary count, Krylov dimension, or wall time at fixed observable error against MPS–HEOM / exact small-N references. Until that benchmark exists, call it a candidate preconditioner, not an advance.

## 4. The computational object worth building: a soft-symmetry compiler

Exact symmetry gives a block decomposition. Near symmetry gives a block matrix
\[
H' = \begin{pmatrix}
H_D & V_{DB}^\dagger \\
V_{DB} & H_B
\end{pmatrix},
\]
where `H_D` is the dark block and `V_DB` vanishes exactly at symmetry. For one twin dark state,
\[
\lVert V_{DB}\rVert = \Delta_\sigma/(2\sqrt2).
\]

The opportunity is to make this **automatic and approximate**, rather than hand-specifying a symmetry:

- graph/hash stage: propose exact and near twin/orbit candidates from weighted neighborhoods;
- algebra stage: certify exact automorphisms or score `Delta_sigma` for candidates;
- basis stage: construct symmetry-adapted odd/even coordinates;
- coupling stage: expose `V_DB` explicitly and compute its numerical rank / singular spectrum;
- dynamics stage: propagate only the bright reachable subspace plus dark singular directions above a tolerance;
- error stage: bound discarded dark-sector influence from `||V_DB||`, time horizon, and relevant spectral gaps;
- open-system stage: perform the same reduction jointly on `H` and the jump-operator algebra.

This is different from ordinary exact symmetry reduction because it treats **measured, imperfect symmetry as an error-controlled low-rank coupling problem**.

## 5. Why spectral impurity and defect should both be kept

At weak nondegenerate breaking, eigenvector perturbation theory gives schematically
\[
L_{spec} \sim \sum_k \frac{|\langle b_k|V|d\rangle|^2}{(E_d-E_k)^2}.
\]
`Delta_sigma` controls the numerator norm; `L_spec` includes the spectral susceptibility. Their ratio therefore diagnoses whether a twin is merely strongly broken or instead weakly broken but nearly resonant with the bright spectrum.

**New K11 diagnostic:** for each twin report the pair

- structural breaking: `Delta_sigma` / `g_DB`;
- spectral susceptibility: `chi_spec := L_spec / g_DB^2` (with a declared floor).

The existing 11.5x leakage asymmetry could then be decomposed into "more symmetry breaking" versus "more resonant bright bath". That is a real unanswered question in the current result.

## 6. PHYS-K11-2 experiments, in spend order

### K2.1 Exact/soft identity gate — cheap

On CUR-P2 and CUR-SP, each twin:

- compute `Delta_sigma` directly on the real `c`;
- compute `g_DB` directly;
- require `|g_DB - Delta/(2sqrt2)|` at machine floor;
- report `L_spec` separately and `chi_spec` only above a numerical floor.

This localizes the source of the 11.5x anchor effect without any new model assumption.

### K2.2 Planted-breaking law — cheap

Start from the group-averaged `c_bar`, inject a frozen perturbation direction `E` with `H(eps)=c_bar+eps E`.

Stake before execution:

- `g_DB proportional to eps`;
- short-time dark survival loss `1-|<d|exp(-iHt)|d>|^2 proportional to eps^2` at fixed small `t`;
- deviations at larger epsilon are reported, not fitted away.

`scratchpad/TRANSITION_MAP/dark_state_softsym.py` is the substrate-independent dye test for this law. A local dry run at n=31 gave exact-tier residual 0, defect/coupling identity error ~1e-16, coupling log-slope 1.000, and short-time survival-loss log-slope ~2.000 under the default weak-breaking window.

### K2.3 Open-system quasi-BIC surrogate — medium

Attach loss/dephasing to selected bright nodes while leaving the symmetry exact, then break the twin symmetry by epsilon. Measure dark-state lifetime / Liouvillian decay mode. Compare quadratic weak-breaking prediction with breakdown near bright-sector resonances.

This ties directly to quasi-BIC physics while staying an explicitly finite/open graph model.

### K2.4 S7 many-body run — already bought by PHYS-K11-1

PHYS-K11-1's S3 map bought S7 and identified the top gauge-invariant loop set. Freeze those targets before the many-body run. Add symmetry-sector bookkeeping so the calculation reports how much many-body response lives in exact/near dark sectors versus the bright sector.

### K2.5 Polariton benchmark — expensive, external SOTA comparison

Use a standard disordered Tavis–Cummings / Holstein–Tavis–Cummings benchmark:

- exact diagonalization / HEOM at small N as truth;
- symmetry-adapted reduction at zero disorder;
- controlled static disorder sweep;
- dynamic disorder / bath correlation-time sweep;
- compare uncompressed and soft-symmetry-preconditioned Krylov/MPS/HEOM calculations at fixed spectra, populations, transfer rates, and error.

Primary performance outputs: wall time, memory, Krylov dimension or MPS bond dimension, and observable error. Primary physics output: bright->dark/gray transfer rate and its dependence on the measured symmetry-defect singular spectrum.

## 7. What could actually be new

Ranked from safest to most ambitious:

1. **Exact diagnostic theorem + software:** automatic equality between swap-conjugation defect and instantaneous dark/bright coupling for Hermitian twin symmetry, integrated with a measured graph pipeline.
2. **Spectral susceptibility decomposition:** separate structural symmetry breaking from resonance amplification using (`Delta_sigma`, `L_spec`) rather than a single leakage number.
3. **Approximate symmetry compiler:** convert near automorphisms into an explicit low-rank dark/bright coupling and an error-controlled reduced propagator.
4. **Open-system joint reduction:** detect approximate invariant sectors of the algebra generated by `H` and Lindblad operators, rather than reducing `H` alone.
5. **Polariton preconditioner:** demonstrate that the above reduction materially extends N, bath complexity, or disorder range at fixed accuracy relative to current MPS–HEOM / tensor-network practice.

Only (5), if benchmarked successfully against current methods, merits a simulation-SOTA claim.

## 8. Falsifiers / anti-hype gates

- If `V_DB` is high-rank and flat-spectrum under realistic disorder, the compiler offers little compression: report that.
- If transformed dissipators densely reconnect the dark manifold, Hamiltonian block reduction does not buy open-system scaling: report that.
- If speedup disappears at fixed error once basis detection / transformations are included, no SOTA claim.
- If `L_spec` variation is dominated by small spectral denominators rather than `Delta_sigma`, the ontology's measured twin-lifting ordering is not primarily a symmetry-breaking-strength effect.
- If polariton benchmarks show tensor networks already exploit the same symmetry/reachability implicitly, the contribution reduces to diagnostics / compiler ergonomics, not asymptotic improvement.
