With both sides in one constraint class, the object supplies a POPULATION of

- object triples: median 0.0548, range [0.0004, 0.2513]

**The quark table's protected pair sits at the 0.3rd percentile of the object's own
population — an extreme outlier.** Its other two pairs (0.0506, 0.0489) sit essentially
AT the object's median (0.0548). **The lepton table's most-protected pair sits at the
51.6th percentile — exactly typical.**

Reading, stated at earned strength: relative to the object, a flavour table is
UNREMARKABLE except for one feature — the quark sector's Cabibbo-scale protection,
which the object's population essentially never produces. That protection is precisely
the near-degeneracy unitarity forces on |V_ud|² and |V_cs|² at small θ13, θ23. So the
bridge localizes what is special about flavour to ONE mechanism, rather than to its
general structure — and the lepton table has no such feature at all.

## Caveats, binding
- The 316 pair-readings come from ONE 11×11 matrix and overlap heavily; the
  percentiles are DESCRIPTIVE positions, not p-values, and no significance is claimed.
- The comparison is of dimensionless diagonal splits in a constraint-matched class.
  It licenses no isomorphism, no shared mechanism, and no revival of `phase-at-ceiling`.
- Sinkhorn is a choice of normalization (the canonical one); a different route to
  double stochasticity could shift the numbers. The GATE result (√3/2 at 4e-13) is
  normalization-independent because it is a theorem.
